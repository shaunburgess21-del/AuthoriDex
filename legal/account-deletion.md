You can ask VoxDex to delete your account in either of these ways:

- **While signed in:** Me → Settings → Account → Delete Account
- **By email:** [legal@voxdex.com](mailto:legal@voxdex.com)

Use [legal@voxdex.com](mailto:legal@voxdex.com) for privacy rights and account-deletion requests. [hello@voxdex.com](mailto:hello@voxdex.com) remains the address for general questions. The contact form still goes to the team for ordinary messages.

## Scheduled deletion is 7 days

Requesting deletion **schedules** it for 7 days. During those 7 days:

- your account stays active
- you can still sign in
- you can cancel the request

The account is not permanently deleted during this window.

## What permanent deletion removes

When the 7 days end, we erase or irreversibly anonymise personal data on active systems:

- sign-in email and authentication identity
- username, avatar, bio, and other direct profile identifiers
- demographics, date of birth, recovery email, phone number, and social handles
- Vox balance, XP, streaks, referrals, preferences, and interests
- sessions and similar account-specific state

Your public profile is removed. It returns not found and is not navigable. The author of your comments is shown as a deleted user, with no profile link.

## What remains, without identifying you

So that historical results stay intact, we keep and disassociate:

- votes
- poll responses
- matchup votes
- predictions

Comment text is replaced with a neutral placeholder (`[deleted]`) so the thread can remain.

The virtual credit ledger is an engineering audit log. Rows may remain because a profile that still has ledger entries cannot be removed. Identifying fields in those rows, in bet metadata, in audit snapshots, and in telemetry are cleared. Vox and predictions are not tax records.

Opinion-poll suggestion text may remain when it is the suggested option rather than your name. The suggester username is cleared.

## Backups

Routine encrypted backups may retain deleted information for up to 60 days before being overwritten.
